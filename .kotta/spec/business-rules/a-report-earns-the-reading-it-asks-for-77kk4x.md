## Rule

A report Kotta prints for a person stays as long as its content, not as long as its subject list. Where many entries share one explanation, the explanation is given once and the entries are named under it. A report never restates the same paragraph per row, and the length of an identical-reason group does not grow with the group.

The summary a reader needs first comes first, and every section below it is skippable without losing a fact the summary promised.

## Rationale

The rule that made admissions carry a kind produced a correct report nobody will finish: 333 lines, 122,078 characters, and one sixty-word paragraph repeated a hundred and eight times, because a wording written for one node was never read back against a hundred of them.

A measure that is not read fails in the same way as a measure that lies. The whole point of counting kept and unkept promises is that someone consults the count before making a promise; a wall of repeated text is consulted once and then skipped, and the number goes back to being decoration.

## Scope

Reports Kotta prints for a person to read — the gap report first among them. Not the machine-readable form, where repetition costs a reader nothing and dropping a field would cost a consumer something: `--json` keeps every entry whole.
