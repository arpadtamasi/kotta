## Rule

An entity's undecided points are enumerated, not narrated. Each open question is one item under the entity's Open decisions heading, addressed by the entity and its position in that list, and it is resolved by naming the decision record that settled it - never by being deleted. A resolved question stays where it stood, so the reasoning survives the answer and answering shifts no other question's position; only writing a new question above an existing one does that, and the position addresses the list as it reads now. The gate that requires no decision to remain open reads that enumeration and nothing else: an entity with no questions passes, an entity whose every question names an existing decision passes, and an entity with an unresolved question is refused naming which ones. The same enumeration is what orientation reports, for one entity or for every entity at once.

## Rationale

Attention is the scarce resource, and a question written as prose cannot be counted, ranked, or answered one at a time - it is found by re-reading the whole entity, or it is not found at all. A free-text denial makes the gate all-or-nothing in the other direction: the entity either claims nothing is open or is refused as a whole, and the reader is never told which point is the one still waiting. Enumerating them turns the entity into something two people can sit down over and work through, and keeping the answered ones in place means the record shows what was asked as well as what was decided, which is what makes the decision legible a month later.

## Scope

Every entity kind whose form carries an Open decisions section, on every surface that reads or gates one. It governs how a question is written, when it counts as answered, and what the defining gate and orientation read. It does not make a question an entity of its own: the question lives in the text of the entity it belongs to and is only made visible. It decides nothing about who answers, which stays the human gate it already is.
