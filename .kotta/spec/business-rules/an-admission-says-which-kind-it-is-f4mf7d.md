## Rule

An admitted gap names which of three situations it is, and the report counts the three apart.

- **structural** — many sites realise the promise and no single one would ever name it. A use case, a goal, an interface, an actor. This is the measurement's boundary, not a debt.
- **unexamined** — nobody has yet looked at whether the promise is kept. An honest placeholder, and the only kind that may be written in bulk.
- **unimplemented** — someone looked, and it is genuinely not built. This is the debt, and the reason says what is missing.

An admission with no kind is refused the way an unadmitted promise is: naming three situations with one word was the defect that made the count unreadable.

## Rationale

One hundred and six admitted gaps carried one sentence between them. Counted as a backlog the number was alarming; taken apart it was three things. Fifty-two were forms no code site names — the instrument measuring itself. The remainder were examples and business rules, and a sample of those turned out to be enforced in code and proven by tests that simply never mention the node: six rules checked, six enforced, with file and line.

A number that lumps "nobody looked" together with "nothing could ever name this" moves for reasons its reader cannot interpret, and a measure nobody can interpret stops being read. Splitting the kinds costs one word per admission and makes the movement mean something.

## Scope

The `accepted` entries `kotta gap` reads, and how the report counts them. Not which kind any particular node deserves — that is a judgement made per node, and assigning one in bulk is exactly what `unexamined` exists to record honestly.
